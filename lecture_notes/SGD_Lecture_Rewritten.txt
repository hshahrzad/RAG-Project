STOCHASTIC GRADIENT DESCENT (SGD)
----------------------------------
Goal: Minimize the average loss over training examples:
    f(w) = (1/N) Σ ℓ(h_w(x_n), y_n)

In practice, computing the full gradient ∇f(w) is expensive for large datasets.

Idea: Use an approximation based on a mini-batch B ⊂ {1, ..., N}:
    ∇f(w) ≈ (1/|B|) Σ ∇_n f_n(w)

Update rule:
    w ← w − η_t * (1/|B|) Σ ∇_n f_n(w)

Extreme case: |B| = 1 (true SGD)

LOGISTIC REGRESSION BY SGD:
----------------------------
Loss: f(w) = (1/N) Σ log(1 + exp(−y_n * wᵗx_n))

Gradient: ∇f_n(w) = −y_n x_n / (1 + exp(y_n * wᵗx_n))

SGD update:
    w ← w − η_t * ∇f_n(w)

WHY SGD WORKS:
---------------
- The stochastic gradient is an unbiased estimator of the full gradient
- Introduces noise that may help escape saddle points and sharp minima

SGD STEP SIZE AND CONVERGENCE:
-------------------------------
- Fixed step size → may not converge
- Step size η_t should decrease to zero (e.g., η_t ~ 1/t^a, a > 0)

LEARNING RATE SCHEDULING:
--------------------------
- Step decay
- Cyclic schedules

SGD VS. GRADIENT DESCENT:
--------------------------
Pros of SGD:
- Lower per-iteration cost
- Faster initial progress

Cons:
- Noisy
- Slower to converge near minima
- Requires learning rate tuning

MOMENTUM:
---------
Update rule:
    v_t = β v_{t−1} + (1−β) ∇f(w_t)
    w_{t+1} = w_t − α v_t

Benefits:
- Smooths noisy updates
- Accelerates convergence

NESTEROV ACCELERATED GRADIENT:
-------------------------------
Look-ahead version:
    v_t = β v_{t−1} + α ∇f(w_t − β v_{t−1})
    w_{t+1} = w_t − v_t

ADAGRAD (2010):
---------------
- Adapts learning rate per coordinate
- Update:
    G_t[j] = G_{t−1}[j] + g_t[j]^2
    w_{t+1} = w_t − η * g_t[j] / sqrt(G_t[j] + ε)

ADAM (2015):
------------
- Combines momentum + adaptive learning rate
- Update:
    m_t = β₁ m_{t−1} + (1−β₁) g_t
    v_t = β₂ v_{t−1} + (1−β₂) g_t²
    m̂_t = m_t / (1 − β₁^t)
    v̂_t = v_t / (1 − β₂^t)
    w_t = w_{t−1} − α * m̂_t / (√v̂_t + ε)

BATCH SIZE AND LARGE SCALE TRAINING:
-------------------------------------
- Larger batch sizes reduce variance, enable parallelism
- But may converge to sharper minima → worse generalization

SHARP VS. WIDE MINIMA:
-----------------------
- Large batch SGD may converge to sharp minima
- Sharp minima generalize poorly

LEARNING RATE SCALING:
-----------------------
- Variance of gradient ~ 1/|B|
- Scale LR with √|B| or |B| to compensate

LAYER-WISE ADAPTIVE LR SCALING (LARS/LAMB):
-------------------------------------------
- Scale learning rate per layer
    w^{(i)} ← w^{(i)} − η * (‖w^{(i)}‖ / ‖g^{(i)}‖) * g^{(i)}
- LAMB uses Adam-style updates instead of raw gradient

Used in training BERT with thousands of GPUs/TPUs.

SUMMARY:
---------
- SGD is a workhorse of modern ML optimization
- Many variants exist: momentum, Nesterov, Adagrad, Adam, LAMB
- Important to choose appropriate learning rate, batch size, and scheduler
